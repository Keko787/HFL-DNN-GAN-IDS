# 5.12's training-time cells at tau = 0.82: reach, and reach at equal flight time

Exploratory, not pre-registered (15 Nov 2026; `scripts/exp5/tau082_horizon.py`). No flights. P9 found two reach claims against F at tau = 0.82, both in these cells at 4 missions; P11 flew them to 10 missions and tested tau = 0.71 only. F, then FX, is the reference against each baseline: reach at 0.82 over the whole run (exact McNemar), reach at 0.82 by the horizon, and the restricted mean time to 0.82 at the horizon (a miss counted as the horizon; the scorer's paired comparison). The horizon is the shortest flight of any arm in any trial of the cell, P11's rule, so every arm has flown to it in every trial. A trial's flight is missions_completed × sim_mission_duration_s_mean, which reproduces P11's trace-based flights (F 925 and 896 s, H1 2,159 and 2,119 s; horizons 436 and 427 s). Holm per run, reference and metric, across the run's two cells; a claim needs the CI to exclude 0 and Holm p < 0.05. Difference = reference − arm: reach, positive when the reference reaches 0.82 more often; time, negative when the reference is faster.

## Reading it

- Over the whole run the baselines reach 0.82 more often, and most of those differences are claims, but the baselines fly 1.8 to 2.6 times as long: F's and FX's missions are shorter, and the run is capped by missions, not by time. P11 found the same cap behind F's reach at 0.71.
- By the horizon, when every arm has flown as long, F and FX reach 0.82 at least as often as every baseline at 10 missions. FX's lead is a claim against H1 and D3 in both cells and against D4 with stragglers, and its restricted time in six of eight comparisons; F's lead is a claim nowhere (Holm 0.06 to 0.43).
- At 4 missions the horizon (209 and 189 s) is too short for almost any arm to reach 0.82, so P9's two claims there show no loss at equal time, and no gain.
- At 10 missions F and FX merge fewer updates per mission (P11.md), since the mule can arrive before training finishes.
