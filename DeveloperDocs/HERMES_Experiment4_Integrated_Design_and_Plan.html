00 · The mandate

Why Experiment 4 exists

Experiment 4 is not in the paper yet. §IV ships two measured groups — the fixed-radio comm baseline (Exp 1) and the scheduling ablation A1–A4 (Exp 3) — plus the preliminary L1 radio validation (Exp 2, Table II). Exp 4 is the one the rebuttal promises next.

The reviewers' sharpest surviving criticism, and the authors' own concession:

From the SEC26 author response

“The three layers are exercised together only in integration tests, not in one end-to-end experiment … those are the honest weaknesses, and they are our first priorities for a revision … build the end-to-end experiment first (it answers the layers-not-integrated concern directly).”

The design already exists on paper. Algorithm 2 is literally titled “Integrated HERMES Mission Round.” It threads all three layers through one round:

Algorithm 2 — one integrated round L1 → chan_idx = L1CHANNELSELECT() ; navigate to c*.pos L2 → c* = SCHEDULERROUND(M, A) (Algorithm 1: gated + RL tie-break) L3 → batch_agg = PARTIALFEDAVG({Δθ_j}) (Pass 1 · COLLECT) θ' = CROSSMULEFEDAVG({mission_agg}) (dock) push θ' to devices (Pass 2 · DELIVER)

Exp 4 is the measured realization of that algorithm: one trial that runs the three real layers and emits both metric families — Exp 1's communication metrics and Exp 3's scheduling metrics — plus new convergence metrics, against a traditional-FL null.

The claim it must support, and the null it must beat

Scientific claim

When L1 (RF-adaptive comm), L2 (mobility-aware gated scheduling + RL selection), and L3 (two-pass hierarchical FedAvg) are composed end-to-end on a UAV data-mule network, integrated HERMES delivers the DNN-IDS to convergence with materially better communication efficiency, scheduling yield/fairness, and mission/propulsion energy than traditional flat FL under the same shaped-radio conditions — and each layer contributes measurably (the layers compose, not merely coexist).

Null (H₀). Integrated HERMES shows no statistically significant improvement (paired Wilcoxon, p ≥ 0.05, negligible Cliff's δ) over traditional FL on the joint metric vector at matched seeds. The paired-seed grid — _derive_seed already omits the arm from the seed (grid.py:175) — is exactly the instrument, just as it is for Exp 1 (FL vs Centralized) and Exp 3 (A1–A4).

Two honesty constraints — verified against the code

No real DNN-IDS runs in any loop today. The FedAvg math is real numpy, but devices “train” via θ + rng.normal(0, 0.01) (processes/device.py:51) and the generator is a 13-param StubGeneratorHost. The create_CICIOT_Model → ClientMission.local_train bridge is an unimplemented stub that raises (TrainingClient.py:192).

L1 is decoupled from L2/L3 at runtime. The selector's RF feature is a hardcoded constant 20.0, never sourced from any live prior. Algorithm 2 line 5 is unrealized outside a single in-process test.

Both resolved (2026-07-24). EX-4.1 puts the real create_CICIOT_Model in the device training loop and aggregates it with the real FedAvg. EX-4.3 ships the U(c,t) controller and feeds the L1-chosen channel's mean SNR into the selector's rf_prior feature, so the 20.0 constant is gone and Algorithm 2 line 5 is realized. See §01.

The single biggest gap

There is no runtime data edge from L1 into L2/L3, and the real orchestrator trains no real model — so the two things Exp 4 exists to demonstrate (that L1 helps L2/L3, and that the integrated stack converges a real DNN-IDS) are both un-instrumented today. Everything else — harness, orchestrator L2+L3 over TCP, real FedAvg math, real policy objects, byte/tx instrumentation points, post-hoc energy — either composes already or is a bounded additive build.

Closed (2026-07-24). Both edges are now built and tested: the real DNN-IDS converges in-loop (EX-4.1) and the L1→L2 rf_prior edge feeds the selector (EX-4.3). What remains genuinely open is the magnitude question — L1's end-to-end contribution is measured at the channel-model level (a ~0.13–0.14 jittery backhaul-loss reduction, ~0 clean) but not yet through the orchestrator; that integrated H2-vs-H3 measurement is the sweep now running.

03 · Evidence

What the code actually shows

Five load-bearing claims the design rests on, each re-checked by an adversarial verifier reading the source directly. These are the facts that make the plan honest rather than aspirational.

CONFIRMED

L1's RF prior is never consumed by the L2 selector at runtime — and the data flow is actually reversed.

RFPriorStore — the design's read-only env API for the selector — is never instantiated or read in runtime code; its readers are called only in test_l1_rf_prior.py. The value the selector actually consumes is the literal 20.0 (SelectorEnv.rf_prior_snr_db default), passed unchanged from every call site. And in _pick_channel_at_position, the constant feeds into the ChannelDDQN state; the DDQN's output is collected into channel_choices and — per its own docstring — “recorded, not actuated.” No measured experiment even instantiates L1.

Implication for Exp 4The L1→L2 edge must be built, not wired: an L1 producer that records real per-band SNR into the store, a scheduler that reads it instead of the constant, and an Exp 4 run driven through MuleSupervisor (with a channel_actor) rather than the L1-less sim. Until then, any “all three layers” claim measures L2+L3 with an L1 stub — the exact limitation the rebuttal admits.
CONFIRMED

Exp 3's measured arms run on the abstracted sim (Bernoulli/byte proxies); real FedAvg lives only in the orchestrator path. No trial today does both.

run_mule_trial instantiates Exp3Sim and models an “update” as completed = rng.random() < p_complete — no tensor, no gradient. Grep across experiments/ for partial_fedavg | cross_mule_fedavg | MultiProcessOrchestrator → “No matches found.” The real weighted FedAvg is reachable only through the subprocess orchestrator (which itself runs a StubGeneratorHost). The two halves of Algorithm 2 are physically separated in code.

Implication for Exp 4New wiring, not a config flip. Either substitute real GradientSubmissions + partial/cross-mule FedAvg into the Exp 3 step (smaller lift — the driver already invokes the real scheduler surface), or drive the orchestrator's HFLHostMission/HFLHostCluster through the L2 scheduler and instrument both metric families.
PARTIAL

The shared harness hosts an Exp 4 driver with zero edits — but the assumed signature and dispatch entry don't exist.

Confirmed: TrialRunner/TrialGrid/CSVTrialLog are fully generic — arbitrary metric columns, schema-union, resumable keys. Both existing drivers plug in identically. Corrected: the contract is single-argument run_trial(cell) → Mapping (arm/seed are fields on the frozen Cell), not run_trial(arm, cell, seed); and there is no --exp dispatcher — each experiment ships its own main(). Exp 4 needs its own entrypoint module, e.g. experiments/exp4/runner_main.py, modeled on Exp 3's.

Implication for Exp 4Harness half is free; the driver body is net-new. It must also choose its scaffold: Exp 1's live-socket server lifecycle or Exp 3's sim loop — the harness hosts either.
CONFIRMED

The energy calibration is internally inconsistent and partly placeholder — a unified Exp 4 energy model cannot reuse both tables as-is.

exp1.ε_bit = 7.0e-10 vs exp3.ε_bit = 1.2e-9 (a 1.714× fork) under a “same constants” comment; the provenance block flags “One of the two is wrong,” status placeholder. ε_prop = 10.0 J/m is REPLACE-FROM-PLATFORM-SPEC and excluded from the ±50% sweep. Both proxies apply bytes×8×ε_bit identically, so the divergence propagates directly. P_idle = 5.0 W is the one consistent constant.

Implication for Exp 4Reconcile ε_bit to one value across L1 radio + L3 mule-transport energy; measure or explicitly declare ε_prop; add an [exp4]/Exp4Calibration table. Until then the provenance machinery correctly watermarks every Exp 4 energy figure.
PARTIAL

Exp 1 shapes real sockets via tc/netem; Exp 3 shapes numbers in-sim. Exp 4 needs one consistent impairment mechanism — and a third, stub, mechanism already exists.

Exp 1: tbf rate 10mbit + netem delay 30ms 9ms loss 2% on a live NIC (note: the “30% jitter” is 30 ms mean latency with 9 ms latency jitter — not bandwidth variation). Exp 3: multiplicative Normal(1,σ) on the upload term only + a per-contact Bernoulli loss — a different kind of impairment. A ChannelEmulator is already wired into the TCP link but is a bandwidth-less stub.

Implication for Exp 4Cleanest single mechanism: drive the real HERMES stack (TCPRFLink) over tc/netem-shaped loopback, so both metric families are emitted under one physically-shaped link — rather than reconciling two numerically-different jitter models.

Provenance: 16-agent extraction + verification workflow over the FL-DNN-GAN-IDS tree. The orchestrator end-to-end capability (below) is established directly from tests/integration/test_e2e_topology.py and test_mule_supervisor_two_pass.py.

04 · Design

Design & the recommended approach

The one true end-to-end path that exists today — test_e2e_topology.py::test_e2e_one_mission_runs_pass_1_dock_pass_2 — drives real subprocesses over real TCP through Pass 1 → dock → Pass 2 with real cross-mule FedAvg (proven by the cluster_round_closed assert). But it wires neither L1 channel selection nor the L2 RL selector, over a stub model. So today's “integrated” test is really L3 + deterministic-gated-L2 only. That test is the seed; the design is about what to add around it.

Three options

OptionShapeTrade-off
1 · Extend the orchestrator to a fully-integrated real runadd channel_actor+target_selector to MuleConfig, real local_train, populate/read RFPriorStore, transport rate-shaping, a round barrier, a JSONL→CSV consumerhighest fidelity, answers the rebuttal head-on, every metric measured — but the largest build, real keras training in M subprocesses dominates runtime, transport shaping is genuinely new code
2 · Hybrid: Exp 1 transport + Exp 3 sim, stitchedtwo engines behind one run_trial: shaped-TCP byte transport for comm metrics, Exp3Sim for scheduling metrics, joined at matched seed into one wide rowfast, reuses two validated engines — but not actually integrated; two decoupled measurements concatenated, exactly what the rebuttal criticizes. L1 still absent, no real convergence. Weakest claim.
3 · Staged layer-ablation over the real stack recommendedOption 1's engine, but the arms are staged so each milestone is independently shippable and closes one gapa genuinely integrated measured trial at every stage; front-loads the cheap high-value wins; defers the expensive/risky legs into isolable milestones so one slip doesn't block the core claim
Recommendation — Option 3

Build the Exp 4 driver on the MultiProcessOrchestrator (the path that already runs end-to-end), and make the staged arms themselves the layer-ablation that demonstrates composition. Each stage below is shippable on its own; the harness, real FedAvg, real policy objects, and post-hoc energy are reused unchanged. Stage 0 already emits a real integrated L2+L3 measurement from the one path that works today.

The one-line takeaway

Exp 4 is mostly an integration + instrumentation effort, not a from-scratch build: the harness, the two-pass orchestrator, real FedAvg math, the real policy objects, and the post-hoc energy proxies already exist. The net-new work is the three verified gaps — L1→L2 wiring, a real model in the loop, and one consistent link-shaping mechanism — plus the driver and analyzer that fold both metric families into one paired-seed trial.

How this was produced. Grounded in the SEC26 paper (§III Algorithm 1/2, §IV metrics & Tables IV–VII), the SEC26 author response, and a 16-agent extraction + adversarial-verification pass over the FL-DNN-GAN-IDS tree. Five of six load-bearing claims were independently verified against source; the orchestrator end-to-end capability is established directly from test_e2e_topology.py and test_mule_supervisor_two_pass.py.
