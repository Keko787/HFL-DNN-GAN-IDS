HERMES Holistic Paper Revision Plan

A consolidated revision strategy derived from the submitted HERMES manuscript, reviewer critiques, professor OKR notes, rebuttal analysis, provenance audit, and the now-completed end-to-end experiment (E4).

Target venue: IEEE ICNC Primary focus: Novelty + evaluation + clarity Drafted 2026-07-23 · Updated 2026-08-13 E4: run, analysed, adversarially audited

Executive Summary

The revision should be treated as a paper rebuild around one defensible thesis, not as a sequence of isolated reviewer patches. The reviewer feedback, professor OKR, rebuttal notes, and current manuscript all converge on the same core problem: HERMES presently risks reading as an integration of useful existing components rather than as a clearly identifiable systems and scheduling contribution.

Recommended central thesis: HERMES enables federated learning to continue across persistently disconnected edge regions by coordinating physical model transport, RF adaptation, mobility-aware scheduling, and hierarchical aggregation without requiring continuous end-to-end reachability.

The revised paper should therefore avoid positioning HERMES as a universal replacement for conventional federated learning. Instead, it should establish HERMES as a targeted DDIL intervention: ordinary federated learning is preferable when infrastructure is healthy, while HERMES becomes valuable when the federation graph is fragmented, connectivity is intermittent, and stationary infrastructure cannot reliably reach the participating devices.

2026-08-13 update — the crossover thesis is now measured, not hypothesised. E4 has been built, run at 20 paired seeds over a 12-point severity surface, and put through an adversarial audit. The predicted crossover held: flat FL wins under a clean backhaul, and HERMES's advantage grows monotonically with terrain blockage to Cliff's δ = +0.98 (p<0.001). That is the paper's payoff result and it is defensible as written.
One planned claim did not survive and must be dropped. The Layer-1 adaptive-radio benefit is not detectable end-to-end: across the H3-vs-H2 sweep, all twenty paired tests are ties. An earlier apparent effect came from a contaminated sweep plus an unpaired effect-size computation, and has been retracted (§7.5). Layer 1 must be presented as a backhaul-robustness mechanism, not an accuracy driver. Sections 7 and 10 are updated accordingly; do not reinstate the older wording.

1. Central Research Claim

The paper should be organized around the missing capability in conventional systems: continued federation when persistent end-to-end connectivity no longer exists.

Domain

DDIL, disaster-response, tactical-edge, and mobile-edge environments with fragmented or unstable connectivity.

Missing Capability

Conventional FL assumes enough ongoing reachability to exchange updates and close rounds.

HERMES Intervention

Use mobile UAV data mules to physically move model state between disconnected regions while adapting communication and scheduling.

Research Payoff

Federation can continue even when stationary infrastructure cannot directly reach every participant.

Capability-level novelty: HERMES should be judged not only by whether a metric improves by a few percent, but by whether the system can perform useful federated coordination in operating regimes where baseline infrastructure-dependent approaches fail to make progress at all.

3. Phase 0 — Correctness and Provenance Audit

This phase must precede substantive rewriting. Several notes identify discrepancies between the manuscript, code, experimental provenance, and calibration assumptions.

3.1 Correct the scheduling equations and behavior

The revised scheduler description should use the implementation-consistent deadline form:

Deadline(j) = t_ref(j) + Φ(j)

A successful clean contact should re-anchor t_ref and shrink the adaptive window, while a failed or partial contact should widen the window and preserve the previous reference. The code now implements this correctly, with unit coverage; what remains is correcting the manuscript text and Algorithm 1, which still print the update with the sign reversed.

3.4 Resolve energy-model calibration [partly done]

Absolute propulsion-energy claims should be avoided until the propulsion coefficient and conflicting communication-energy constants are calibrated consistently. Until then, report relative or normalized energy rather than treating absolute joules per update as platform-measured quantities.

Status: the ε_bit fork is resolved — E1 and E3 both used to carry different per-bit costs (7.0×10−10 vs 1.2×10−9 J/bit) under a comment claiming they were the same physics, so no single trial could emit a comm-transmit and a mission-transmit energy consistently. Both are now 7.0×10−10 and marked verified. ε_prop remains a placeholder (10.0 J/m), so the aggregate provenance status is still placeholder and every energy figure keeps its watermark. Normalized energy remains the only reportable form for ICNC.

3.5 Fix implementation issues before publishing stronger scheduling claims

  • Ensure the “new node” trust/reachability gate can eventually close.
  • Prevent the feasibility-aware baseline from pathological over-pruning or explicitly reframe it as a cautionary arm.
  • Clear deadline overrides correctly after cluster amendments.
  • Add an upper bound to Φ to support bounded/stability claims.
  • Separate PARTIAL and TIMEOUT semantics if they are intended to represent different outcomes.
  • Integrate the existing reliability statistic into the actual scheduling decision if reliability/trust is claimed.
  • Verify that readiness filtering is active in production code before claiming its experimental effect.

4. Turn Layer 2 into the Identifiable Methodology Contribution

4.1 Separate hard constraints from learned optimization

The scheduler should be presented as a gated decision process rather than as “EDF plus DQN.” Deterministic rules should first establish which choices are legal; learning should only rank among already-valid choices.

Core architectural guarantee: the learned policy cannot override mission-critical hard constraints.

Recommended decision pipeline:

  1. Eligibility gate — device belongs to the mission or is admitted opportunistically.
  2. Readiness gate — a useful local update is actually available.
  3. Reachability / feasibility gate — contact is physically and temporally achievable.
  4. Mission-budget gate — travel + contact + return can fit within the remaining budget.
  5. Priority / deadline ordering — deterministic ordering identifies the active candidate set.
  6. Bounded RL tie-break — the learned selector acts only within the allowed candidate set.

4.2 Make the scheduling objective explicit

The professor's OKR requires energy, distance, readiness, trust/reliability, and other operational constraints to be visible in the methodology. A revised scoring or reward model could take the form:

U_j = w_r R_j + w_t T_j + w_q Q_j + w_u U_j − w_d D_j − w_e E_j − w_τ C_j
TermInterpretation
R_jModel/update readiness.
T_jObserved reliability or trust based on contact history.
Q_jRF/link quality or RF prior.
U_jFederated-learning urgency or expected update utility.
D_jTravel distance or route cost.
E_jExpected propulsion and communication energy.
C_jContact/deadline cost.

Important: this multi-objective formulation is a proposed revision direction. It should not be claimed as implemented until the corresponding code and experiments exist.

4.3 Formalize deadline adaptation

The paper should explicitly define:

  • every scheduling variable and its units;
  • clean, partial, timeout, and unreachable outcomes;
  • the exact adaptation update for each outcome;
  • minimum and maximum adaptive-window bounds;
  • why failure widens the scheduling window;
  • why reliable service shrinks it;
  • how adaptation magnitudes are chosen; and
  • sensitivity to those choices.

4.4 Clarify bucket semantics

Priority buckets should be described as candidate provenance / admission classes, not vaguely as urgency or expected FL quality. Recommended semantics:

  1. NEW — never successfully served.
  2. SCHEDULED_THIS_ROUND — assigned to the current mission slice.
  3. BEACON_ACTIVE — recently detected and opportunistically serviceable.

Urgency should then be determined by deadline order within the selected bucket, while RL acts only as a bounded tie-breaker.

5. Reposition HFL as Core System Infrastructure

Layer 3 should no longer be presented mainly as a standard aggregation component. Instead, hierarchical federated learning should be defined as the state-distribution, synchronization, and data-transport substrate that makes physical federation possible.

Non-blocking Federation

A round closes over available mission aggregates rather than stalling forever on an unreachable participant.

Hierarchical Scalability

Updates are aggregated at device/contact/mule levels before cross-mule aggregation at the cluster.

Model-State Consistency

The explicit collect → aggregate → redistribute lifecycle reduces uncontrolled asynchronous drift.

Physical Model Transport

Model updates and refreshed global state are carried across disconnected regions rather than assuming a live end-to-end graph.

Recommended wording direction: HERMES does not claim a novel aggregation operator. Its novelty lies in how hierarchical aggregation is used as infrastructure to coordinate distributed model state across intermittently reachable regions.

6. GAN Decision Gate [DEFERRED — not in the ICNC submission]

Decision: deferred, not dropped. The GAN direction is held out of the ICNC submission — the page budget cannot carry a second algorithmic thread alongside the gated scheduler, and no reviewer asked for one. The section is retained here because the decision gate below is the thing that matters: it defines what would have to be true for the GAN to earn its place, so the question can be reopened for the journal/extended version without re-litigating it from scratch. Nothing in the ICNC paper should reference it.

The professor OKR proposes elevating the GAN component to strengthen algorithmic novelty. This should only be done if the GAN addresses a HERMES-specific failure mode.

6.0 What would have to be true to un-defer it

Concretely, the GAN becomes worth including when all three of these hold — and E4 now gives a cheap way to test the first two:

  1. The failure mode is real in our own data. E4 already surfaces a candidate: sessions that receive no aggregated update end at the untrained initialization (~1.9 % of trials overall, 3.35 % for flat FL under jitter). If devices in blocked regions are also class-starved — not merely unreachable — that is a HERMES-specific learning failure a generative augmentation could address. Currently unmeasured: we have not characterized per-device class distributions in the dead-zone cells.
  2. Augmentation measurably repairs it under the same paired-seed protocol as §7.4, with the CI-excludes-zero rule. A GAN that helps on average but ties under the protocol is not a contribution.
  3. It survives the same audit E4 got. The layer-1 retraction (§7.5) is the cautionary precedent: a component that is wired and plausible is not the same as a component with a demonstrable end-to-end effect.
PathWhen to Use ItBenefit
Preferred: Scheduler as primary algorithmic contribution If the gated scheduler is sufficiently formalized, extended, and validated with sensitivity, scale, and ablation analysis. Keeps the paper focused and directly answers the EDF/heuristic critique.
Optional: GAN-enhanced resilient learning If disconnected devices suffer sparse, non-IID, incomplete, or missing-class observations and GAN augmentation measurably improves robustness. Creates a second algorithmic contribution tied directly to constrained learning.
Avoid adding a GAN merely to “have another algorithm.” RF + UAV + scheduler + HFL + IDS + GAN without a single coherent failure mode would reinforce the reviewers' concern that the work is an integration of many existing technologies.

7.1 E1 should be demoted rhetorically

FL versus centralized raw-data transfer is structurally expected to favor FL on communication volume. Keep this experiment as an architectural microbenchmark validating why compact model updates are suitable for DDIL operation, but do not treat it as a competitive HERMES result.

7.2 E2 should become a reproducible RF-layer validation

Preserve the distinction between deployable adaptive selection and the retrospective static oracle. If possible, use multiple mobility/SNR traces or repeated trace segments so the result is not dependent on a single captured trajectory.

7.3 E3 should become the main algorithm experiment

Expand the scheduling study to include:

  • larger device populations and, if practical, multiple mule counts;
  • component ablations for each gate and each reward/state feature;
  • deadline-adaptation sensitivity;
  • trust/reliability and readiness sensitivity;
  • easy / moderate / hard operating regimes;
  • worst-quartile and variance analysis in addition to mean results;
  • paired Wilcoxon tests and Cliff's δ effect sizes; and
  • SOTA-inspired policy baselines implemented under the same simulator and seeds.
Preferred RL finding: the learned selector is not necessary under unconstrained conditions; its benefit becomes significant when budgets are tight, links are degraded, and multiple superficially similar candidates must be distinguished.

7.4 E4 is the paper's payoff experiment — and it delivered

E4 is built and run: the full arm ladder H0 → H1 → H2 → H3 executes on the real multi-process orchestrator (real subprocesses, real TCP, real two-pass hierarchical FedAvg) with a real DNN-IDS training in every device process. 20 paired seeds across a dead_zone × link_quality surface, canonical CIC-IoT-2023. The predicted crossover is confirmed, and it is conditional — which is the stronger claim.

The headline for the revision: under a healthy backhaul, centralized flat FL is strictly better — the mule is pure overhead, and the paper should say so plainly. As devices lose their long-range path, HERMES's advantage grows monotonically, from a tie to decisive (Cliff's δ up to +0.98, p<0.001 at 60 % blockage). The crossover boundary sits around dead-zone ≈ 0.2–0.4. This is exactly the “targeted DDIL intervention” framing of the Executive Summary, now with numbers behind it.

Report the flip, do not bury it

At the well-connected corner (dead-zone 0.0, link quality 0.7) the surface flips: H0 significantly beats H1. The reviewer critique explicitly asked whether such a flip exists; it does, and reporting it is what makes the rest of the surface credible.

State the mechanism, not just the AUC gap

Decomposing the pooled jittery AUC advantage (+0.023): 76 % is session-survival rate (H0's session collapses 3.35 % of the time vs H1's 0.84 %) and only 24 % is model quality. Conditional on the session training at all, the AUC difference is +0.007 with a CI of [−0.001, +0.015] — a tie by our own claim rule. So the honest sentence is “the mule's sessions survive,” not “the mule trains a better model.” Lead with participation and round-close; report AUC as the downstream consequence.

7.5 E2 / Layer 1 — the adaptive-radio claim must be scaled back

Retracted: an earlier layer-1 result appeared to show adaptive band selection improving model quality at every blockage level (Cliff's δ +0.14…+0.37). A clean re-run (200/200 valid trials, 20/20 paired seeds per cell) shows all 5 conditions × 4 metrics are ties, two nominally negative. The earlier reading came from a contaminated sweep combined with an unpaired effect size computed against a paired protocol. Do not reuse those numbers.

What survives, and is defensible: the U(c,t) controller — which the audit found was specified in the paper but never actually shipped as runtime code — now exists, is wired end-to-end (including the L1→L2 RF-prior edge that was previously a hardcoded constant), and measurably reduces modelled mule→base-station backhaul loss under a jittery, band-crossing channel (≈0.13–0.14 at our calibration; adaptive never worse across 1000 seeds; ≈0 on a healthy channel, correctly).

Recommended framing for ICNC: present Layer 1 as a backhaul-robustness mechanism, not an accuracy driver. If asked for end-to-end evidence, the honest answer is that the integrated effect is small and we could not resolve it at this sample size — one cell at a 6-mission horizon reaches significance, a five-cell sweep at a 4-mission horizon does not. A candid negative result here costs little and protects the two claims that are strong (C1 capability, C2 scheduling).

7.6 Figure and statistical standards (now enforced in code)

Two figure defects were caught during E4 and both are the kind a reviewer finds first. The standards below are now implemented in a shared module (experiments/analysis/figstyle.py) rather than left to convention, and are recorded in the methodology document’s §5.1.

RuleWhy
Figures take their numbers from the same module that produces the tables A layer-1 figure was built from a different sweep than the table it illustrated and disagreed with it by ~0.19 AUC. Shared code makes that impossible.
Uncertainty is a percentile bootstrap CI of the mean — never ±SD Final AUC is bimodal (a session either trains or stays at its untrained initialization), so ±SD implies a spread that does not exist and rendered above AUC = 1.0 — an impossible value that shipped in a committed figure.
All metrics normalized to [0,1]; shared axes across figures Updates/round was a raw count on a panel adjacent to a fraction. Normalizing and fixing one difference-axis range means a null cannot look as dramatic as a large effect.
Truncated axes are declared, and nothing is cropped The AUC panels are zoomed to [0.60, 1.00] for readability, with break marks, a caption stating bar lengths are not proportional, and below-window sessions shown as explicit counts. The floor is 0.60 because 0.80 would have cropped a real bar mean and four CI bounds.
A claim requires the CI to exclude 0 and p<0.05 Several E4 cells have p<0.05 with a CI spanning zero. Under a single-criterion rule they would have been reported as wins.
Invalid trials fail loudly; excluded rows are counted in the output Trials that produced no model were recorded as successes and silently skewed the means.

8. State-of-the-Art Comparison Plan

The absence of recent UAV-FL baselines was raised repeatedly and should not remain an acknowledged gap in the resubmission.

8.1 Baseline families to represent

  • resource-aware FL client selection;
  • utility-guided FL participant selection;
  • UAV-enabled FL trajectory/resource-allocation methods;
  • freshness / Age-of-Information-aware UAV-FL scheduling;
  • multi-community UAV-aided FL; and
  • relevant mobile-relay or semi-decentralized FL approaches.

8.2 Preferred comparison methodology

Where feasible, reimplement the scheduling logic or decision principles of the competing methods inside the same HERMES evaluation harness. This keeps mobility, RF conditions, seeds, mission budgets, and metrics controlled.

8.3 Revise the SOTA feature table

Avoid an overly broad “everyone is ×, HERMES is ✓ everywhere” presentation. Compare narrower, defensible capabilities instead:

  • persistent-connectivity assumption;
  • physical model transport;
  • adaptive relay scheduling;
  • RF-aware scheduling input;
  • hierarchical / multi-stage aggregation;
  • disconnected federation support;
  • hard-constrained learned selection.

9. Revised Manuscript Structure

I. Introduction

Use the requested progression: Domain → Problem → Challenge → Existing Limitation → HERMES → What Becomes Possible → Contributions.

II. Related Work and Capability Gap

Organize prior work by assumptions and supported capabilities, not merely by technology category.

III. System Model and Design Requirements

  • DDIL connectivity model;
  • stationary server reachability;
  • mule/device/contact assumptions;
  • readiness and reliability/trust definitions;
  • model-state lifecycle; and
  • energy/mission-budget model.

IV. HERMES Architecture

Retain the three-layer architecture because reviewers generally found the layered decomposition understandable. Correct all implementation labels and use the architecture diagram as the visual backbone of the system explanation.

V. Gated Federation Scheduling Methodology

Promote the scheduler from a buried implementation subsection into its own major methodological section, with formal equations, gates, algorithm, complexity, invariants, and design rationale.

VI. Hierarchical Federation and Model Transport

Explain the collect → partial aggregate → cross-mule aggregate → redistribute lifecycle and the infrastructure properties it enables.

VII. Experimental Methodology

Begin with a provenance table showing which results are real hardware/cloud, digital twin, trace replay, simulation, or full multi-process execution.

VIII. Results

Organize results as E1 → E2 → E3 → E4. Each subsection should begin with a research question and end with a direct answer.

IX. Discussion and Limitations

  • when HERMES helps;
  • when ordinary FL is preferable;
  • simulation-to-real limitations;
  • calibration limits;
  • multi-UAV scale limits; and
  • what remains future work.

X. Conclusion

Re-state the capability enabled by HERMES rather than summarizing the three layers as independent improvements.

10. Fitting the Paper to IEEE ICNC

ICNC is a computing/networking/communications venue, which suits HERMES better than a machine-learning venue would: the contribution readers will reward is the networked-systems capability — sustaining federation without end-to-end reachability — with the IDS model as the workload that proves it, not as the contribution itself.

Confirm against the current CFP before committing. Page limit, template (IEEE conference two-column), anonymity policy, and deadlines should be read off the official ICNC call rather than assumed from previous years. The guidance below is about what to include, not about format specifics.

10.1 What to lead with at this venue

  • Lead with the capability and the crossover. “Federation continues where infrastructure cannot reach, and here is exactly where that starts paying off” is a networking result. The dead-zone ≈ 0.2–0.4 boundary is the single most quotable number in the paper.
  • Lead with participation, not accuracy. Round-close rate, update yield and mission completion are the systems metrics an ICNC audience reads natively — and per §7.4 they are also where the effect genuinely lives. AUC follows as the downstream consequence.
  • Keep the scheduler as the algorithmic core (C2). The gated-decision framing with a bounded RL tie-break answers the “this is just EDF” critique in the language of a networking reviewer: hard constraints first, learning only within the feasible set.
  • Keep the provenance table. Distinguishing Chameleon Cloud, AERPAW digital twin, simulation, and full multi-process execution is unusually rigorous for a 6-page venue and directly defuses the “real vs simulated” critique.

10.2 What to cut for a short-format venue

A tight page budget forces a choice, and the E4 results make that choice easy. Suggested priority if space is short:

KeepCompressCut or defer
E4 crossover figure + surface table; gated scheduler formalization; E3 scheduling ablation; provenance table E1 to a few sentences of motivation (it is a microbenchmark, not a competitive result); E2 to the channel-level claim only Defer the GAN direction (§6) to the journal/extended version — it adds a second algorithmic thread the page budget cannot support and no reviewer asked for it; the decision gate is kept on file with the criteria that would un-defer it. Cut absolute energy figures until ε_prop is measured

10.3 Two candid statements that will help, not hurt

  1. “Ordinary FL is better under healthy infrastructure.” Stating the clean-regime loss (δ −0.69 on AUC, −0.96 on participation) up front makes the jittery result credible. A reviewer who finds that concession themselves will distrust everything else.
  2. “The adaptive-radio layer's end-to-end benefit was not resolvable at our sample size.” Reporting the L1 null (§7.5) is cheap — the paper's claims rest on C1 and C2 — and it signals the kind of rigor that makes the strong results believable.
Single-sentence pitch to aim the abstract at: HERMES sustains federated learning across regions that stationary infrastructure cannot reach, by physically transporting model state on UAV data mules under a gated, mobility-aware scheduler — and we show measurably where that stops being overhead and starts being necessary.

13. Submission Success Criteria

The revision is ready when all of the following are true:

  • A reader can identify the problem, missing capability, HERMES solution, and main novelty from the first page.
  • The scheduling methodology is independently describable as a research contribution.
  • Every scheduling variable and update rule is justified and empirically tested.
  • HFL is clearly presented as system infrastructure, not merely a standard aggregation block.
  • The paper contains four claim-driven experiments with distinct research questions.
  • The end-to-end experiment reports actual learning quality and convergence.
  • Recent UAV-FL scheduling approaches are represented as fair baselines.
  • Simulation, digital twin, cloud execution, trace replay, and real-system execution are clearly distinguished.
  • All reported tables and figures are reproducible and their provenance is known.
  • Energy claims are appropriately calibrated or explicitly normalized.
  • RL is presented as regime-dependent rather than universally superior.
  • The final paper explicitly states when ordinary FL is better and when HERMES becomes necessary.
  • Every figure draws its numbers from the same code that produces the tables, so the two cannot disagree.
  • No axis renders a value the metric cannot take, and any truncated axis is declared with break marks and a caption.
  • A claim is made only where the confidence interval excludes zero and p<0.05; cells failing either test are reported as ties.
  • Null results are reported rather than dropped — specifically the Layer-1 end-to-end null (§7.5).
  • Invalid trials cannot masquerade as successes: a run that produced no model is recorded as such and excluded from every metric, not just the ones it left blank.
Final positioning: Conventional FL is preferable when reliable infrastructure already exists. HERMES's contribution is making useful federation continue when that assumption fails. Its gated scheduler determines which physically reachable model contributions are worth acquiring under mobility and resource constraints; its HFL substrate moves and reconciles those contributions without blocking on absent devices; and its RF layer improves the quality of temporary communication opportunities.
