Bottom line

FeRRy is the Path A system the contribution ledger calls HERMES; hermes/ is the package it gets built in. The plan below was checked against the code at 229a093, and the code changes the cost picture in three places the ledger did not see.

The build

Seven phases, one learned object

Repair the ground truth, ship the age-weighted merge and the channel-free baselines, then build the simulated clock and contact link, the plan clock, and the flight-time (band, stop) score. Everything except that score stays deterministic or exhaustive.

Before any new code

Two recorded results need a re-run

The backhaul loss schedule was never indexed past mission 1, which narrows what the L1 confirmation measured. And an empty mission never refreshed the budget stamp, so later missions planned against a shrinking budget; in the 60 s cell 72–81% of missions were empty. Both are now fixed.

Costlier than the ledger says

Age is not observable yet

No θ version travels with an update, so neither merge knows an update's age, and Pass 2 re-delivers θ to the whole slice, so ages barely spread. C3's cutoff needs a version on four message types and a budgeted Pass 2.

Decide first

Band classes by bandwidth, not carrier

3.32, 3.34 and 3.90 GHz differ by at most 1.4 dB of free-space loss, so a range trade has to come from channel width. With an 18.8 KB model, dwell only matters on narrow classes or larger payloads, so payload size is a decision too.

The index was therefore always 0, and every --l1-channel run applied mission 1's loss to every mission.   2. Rules the build keeps

  • Hard gates stay deterministic. S1, the S3b predicate and the age cap run before anything learned, and the pair score only ranks inside the admitted set. scope_guard.py checks pairs the way it checks contacts today. This is design principle 12 in spirit.
  • The mule never inspects payloads. It weights updates by metadata (n, reported loss, basis version), and in agg:seq it forwards θ without reading it (principles 9 and 10).
  • The cluster never sees an individual update (principle 3); missions stay two-pass (13); local training stays offline and sessions stay exchange-only (14).
  • hermes/ stays numpy-only. The clock, channel and contact-link code live in hermes/l1/, so FerrySim and the stack share them without hermes importing experiments (finding A-01).
  • Three principles change, and Freeze Amendment 7 records how: 1 (one job per layer) and 5 (only the waypoint crosses from L2 to L1) because band now enters the next-stop decision, and the letter of 12 because a (band, stop) choice is more than ordering within a bucket. The same amendment keeps the frozen pipeline as a legacy mode, on by default, so every recorded arm can still run.

One mission, end to end

What the finished system does from one dock to the next, for one mule over its slice. The numbering is the order of events.

  1. Plan clock · at the dock, once per mission
  2. Take the DOWN bundle.θ with its global version v, the mission slice, deadline amendments and each device's per-band SNR signature. The simulated clock resets and the mule's pose returns to the dock.
  3. Build demand.S1 eligibility, then for each device Deadline(j) = t_ref(j) + Φ(j), a value proxy v_j from its last reported loss and example count, its age a_j in cluster rounds, and a cap flag once a_j reaches S. A device that missed carries a priority boost that is separate from its deadline window.
  4. Cluster once per band class.For each class b̄, S3a groups the eligible devices into stops at range R(b̄). A narrower, longer-reach class makes fewer, farther stops and slower sessions.
  5. Search and score.Every (b̄, π) candidate gets V(b̄, π): the FedEx-form term in tour time Δ = travel + Σ dwell at the class's predicted rate + upload, plus a coverage penalty for each device it leaves out. Exhaustive up to 6 stops, 2-OPT above.
  6. Gate.S3b keeps a candidate only if every stop's transit + session + return + upload fits min(Deadline(j), budget) and the energy clause holds. A candidate that drops a capped device loses to any feasible one that keeps it.
  7. Commit.(b̄, ordered queue, budget) for this mule's slice. Path B+ is this step with b̄ pinned to one class.
  8. Flight clock · at every arrival
  9. Fly.The clock advances by distance over cruise speed. Nothing sleeps in real time.
  10. Arrive and observe.Read the SNR on each band at this position and time from the channel model, not the plan's prediction.
  11. Choose (band, next stop).One score over the admitted pairs, at most 3 bands × 6 stops = 18, with the pairs the predicate rejects at the observed rate masked out. FeRRy fills this slot with the learned pair Q; FX fills it with the best-rate band and the nearest feasible stop. (As built in Phase 5: the pairs are the classes that reach every device b̄ reaches at this stop, times the stops left (home only once none is), and 18 is not a bound (up to 15 pairs at N = 12 and 21 at N = 24 in the probes). A pair is admitted only while the whole rest of the flight still fits after this stop, priced at the observed SNR; the band serves this stop at once, and the chosen stop moves to the front of the remainder, which the departure check then folds. With no pair admitted the mule flies FX's pair. FX, built in Phase 4, takes the fastest class that still reaches b̄'s devices.)
  12. Serve.Solicit only the stop's devices within R(b); push θ_v; collect each Δθ with its basis version, example count and local loss. Dwell = bytes / rate(b, SNR) is charged to the clock.
  13. Check and re-plan.Re-run the predicate on the remaining queue at the observed rate. If it fails, drop or reorder with 2-OPT under the predicate, capped stops first, instead of aborting. A device that beacons in joins the queue only if the predicate admits it.
  14. Merge clock · at the deadline, then at the dock
  15. Merge on the mule.θ_mule = θ_base + Σ w_i·Δθ_i with w_i ∝ n_i·v_i·s(age_i), and w_i = 0 past a_max. Each device's age, band and outcome go into the round report, and the fast-phase deadline update applies.
  16. Merge across mules at the dock.The cluster applies the same weight with age in cluster rounds, mixes the result into θ instead of overwriting it, and issues θ_{v+1} with slow-phase amendments.
  17. Deliver inside the budget.Pass 2 carries θ_{v+1} out. Devices the mule does not reach keep training on the older base, and their next update carries that age.
  18. Log.Per-device ages, Network AoU, deadline misses, round closure and time to τ go to the traces. FerrySim computes the pair Q's reward from the same quantities: r_k = Σ w_dl·s(age)·v − c_t·Δt − c_e·ΔE − c_miss·[miss], with the coverage penalty at the end of the episode. (As built in Phase 5, decision 4: r_k is the L3 weight of the updates collected at stop k over n_ref·N, minus c_t·Δt_k/T_nom, with c_cov times the weighted share of committed devices left uncollected at each sortie's last decision; c_e = 0, no lateness or miss term, and a sortie is the Q's horizon.)

Phased plan

The order follows the ledger's dependency argument with the code's corrections folded in. Phase 0 repairs what later phases would otherwise inherit. Phases 1 and 2 need nothing from the channel model, so their conclusions carry over when Phase 3 changes what every trial does. Phases 4 and 5 add one decision per clock. D1 · gates Phase 3

Band classes and their range–rate model

Define classes by channel bandwidth and MCS on one carrier. The AERPAW carriers differ by 0.05 dB (3.32 vs 3.34 GHz) and 1.40 dB (3.32 vs 3.90 GHz) of free-space loss, too little to carry a range trade. Narrowing a channel from 20 MHz to 1.4 MHz lowers the noise floor by about 11.5 dB, which stretches range about 2.4× at a path-loss exponent of 3, for a proportionally lower peak rate. Anchor the numbers to measured AERPAW ranges where they exist; otherwise cite the model and its parameters.

Accepted 29 Sep. Three classes on 3.32 GHz: wide 20 MHz (100 RB), medium 5 MHz (25 RB) and narrow 1.4 MHz (6 RB). R_planar(wide) = rf_range_m = 60 m, n = 2.2, h = 25 m, σ_sh = 4 dB with a 90 % edge margin, which gives 60, 119 and 232 m planar; Phase 3 flies wide. No measured AERPAW range exists, so the anchor is a declared assumption; it implies an EIRP of about −11 dBm (configuration reference §17.1).

without it, C1 is a knob, not a claim
D2 · gates Phase 3

Seconds-axis channel and the re-run bill

Index SNR by simulated seconds with a period per band, so that transit time over channel period can be swept for test (c). Noise must be a seeded function of time so that seeds stay paired. Accept the bill: the checklist's rule is that anything that changes what a trial does forces a re-run, which here means every mule arm.

Accepted 29 Sep, with the period common to the classes (a deviation from the text above). The contact SNR is the mean plus shadowing (σ 4 dB, 7.4 s) plus an interference term with one period for every class (60 s, swept in test (c)) and a seeded phase per class, clean by default whatever the cell's regime. The backhaul keeps today's model with the mission index replaced by t / (n_missions · T_nom). Noise is a hashed function of time and outcomes are keyed by (seed, key, round). The seconds backhaul is chosen per study: it loses about 16 % of uploads when jittery at a fixed carrier.

today's channel is indexed by mission, one cycle per trial
D3 · gates Phase 3

Payload size and energy

The IDS model is about 18.8 KB per direction, so at Mb/s rates its dwell is a fraction of a second and the dwell term only bites on narrow classes. Report the real payload and sweep 18.8 KB, 1 MB and 10 MB (Exp 3's clean and jittery sizes). ε_prop = 10 J/m is still a placeholder: calibrate it, or label every energy figure simulated.

Accepted 29 Sep. θ measures 18,756 B at the canonical 21 inputs (a Pass-1 session 37,576 B), and a payload declared per direction (1 MB, 10 MB) drives dwell and upload. Energy is the Zeng–Xu–Zhang 2019 rotary-wing model (143.6 W at 5 m/s, 168.5 W hovering), labelled simulated, with the capacity clause off; ε_prop stays in Exp 3.

D4 · gates Phase 4

Age cap S against the budget

Too small an S makes every plan infeasible under a 30 s budget. Choose the smallest S for which the Phase 4 pilot finds a covering schedule at both budgets, and report cap violations as a metric instead of hiding them.

Accepted 30 Sep. The age counts the missions the device's own mule has flown since its last merged update (a device never merged counts from mission 0), the scorer's unit, not cluster rounds. S is the smallest value that covers 90 % of layouts at both pilot budgets (the knee and the stress budget), never below 2, computed per cell by the S* tool at planning level, which also prints S + 1; violations are reported by cause, with no pass mark. A capped device that its S3a stop cannot serve alone within the budget is offered a one-device stop at its best hover point (decided the same day, after the final check). At 1 MB and the prior budgets of 90 and 45 s the tool gives S = 2 for F (configuration reference §18.2 and §18.7).

Non-goals

  • No rebranding as asynchronous FL. The survey taxonomy would absorb it and the comparison would shrink to FedAsync against partial FedAvg.
  • No motion planning or obstacle avoidance. Navigation means next-stop selection and re-planning over known device positions.
  • No hybrid-action RL unless dwell, speed or power become continuous, and no hierarchy unless test (b) shows a slope on both clocks.
