The prior problem

Before any redesign: the reward is flat

The layer question is downstream of a measurement problem that will defeat any architecture you choose. It is already visible in the matrix results, stated as a strength — which it is — without the consequence being drawn.

H1's final_auc sits between 0.922 and 0.933 in every one of the twelve cells — flat. H0's falls from 0.943 to 0.587 as the infrastructure degrades.

That flatness is the headline finding: the mule is insensitive to backhaul degradation because it never uses the backhaul that is failing. It is also the reason no policy operating inside the mule arm can demonstrate anything. The mule already saturates the outcome. There is almost no variance left for a scheduler to explain, which is exactly why H2 ties H1 — and exactly what the C-sweep found on its own when its prediction failed:

The mechanism

A layer is additive. A coupling is not.

The reason "trajectory navigation" reads as just another layer is that, as specified, it is one. Three modules that each optimise their own objective and hand a result downstream compose into a chain — and a chain decomposes, which means a cross-heuristic solves it, which means RL is unjustified. Correctly.

But the physics of this system is not a chain. It is a cycle, and three of its edges are currently severed in code.

  1. 1

    Band does not set range

    In physics, band determines range and penetration and rate — a low band buys reach at the cost of throughput, a high band inverts it. In the code they are independent: rf_range_m is a fixed 60 m constant regardless of which band L1 selects, so band choice cannot change who shares a contact waypoint, and therefore cannot change the route.

    experiments/exp4/driver.py:75,155 — rf_range_m default 60.0, no band dependence

  2. 2

    Arrival time does not set the rate you find

    The RF prior reaching the selector is mean_chosen_snr_db — one mission-mean scalar, identical across every candidate in a ranking batch. The channel is time-varying by construction, so the quantity that actually matters is the phase at the moment the mule arrives, which is a function of the route. Today that link does not exist, and it is one reason four of the eleven feature slots are constant within every batch.

    channel.py → MuleConfig.rf_prior_snr_db → selector/features.py:49 slot 10 · L2 record §4.6

  3. 3

    Dwell does not depend on the rate

    Partially severed, and worth stating precisely: S3b does model travel at cruise_speed_m_s and does test the mission budget. What it does not model is dwell — session_time is a constant 1.0 s, so a slow band and a fast band cost the same time at a stop. That constant is what stops rate from feeding back into feasibility, and it is an uncited platform placeholder besides.

    hermes/scheduler/stages/s3b_feasibility.py · Freeze D2 (session_time = 1.0 s, cruise = 5.0 m/s)

The sharpest case for learning

You already prototyped the right structure

hermes_rl/drone_env.py has the joint action — (waypoint, base station, channel) — with transit consuming time and carrying no transfer, and alpha > beta so the time-varying term dominates distance. Its parameters put it squarely in the regime where myopic scoring is structurally wrong, not merely mistuned.

This is the strongest RL argument available to you, and it is the one to build the experiment around: the agent must choose a destination based on what the channel will be when it gets there. Value functions exist for exactly that. Make the ratio of transit time to channel period an explicit swept parameter — it is the knob that turns the learned policy's advantage on and off, and a sweep over it produces a crossover curve rather than a single contested number.

The five questions

Answers

Q1

Should we include a trajectory navigation layer?

Yes — half of it

Split what the doc lists. Distance and order: add. That is travel cost, and Exp 4 currently has none of it — no flight budget, travel time, propulsion or communication energy anywhere in the path. You have the machinery in Exp 3's sim_env and in S3b; it is disconnected, not missing.

Environment and obstacles: don't. Obstacle avoidance is solved motion planning. A reviewer will say "use A* or RRT" and be right, and it adds no FL-relevant structure — an obstacle changes your path length, which you could obtain by sampling path lengths. It buys complexity without buying coupling, which is the specific failure mode you are trying to escape.

Q2

Should the navigation be dynamic?

Yes — one sense only

Dynamic in the sense of re-planning when the world diverges from the plan, not reacting to terrain. You already have the seed: MuleSupervisor._remaining_is_feasible() re-runs the S3b check from the mule's current pose and clock before each stop. Today it aborts. Make it re-plan.

Your L2 record already draws the boundary correctly, and it should survive into the paper: running out of time is a deterministic function of clock and geometry and can be foreseen; a random link failure is stochastic by construction and cannot. That is a property of the model, not a gap in it.

Q3

Should we jointly optimise RF band and navigation?

Yes — and this is the contribution

Yes, but the joint variable has to be the one that carries the coupling. Not "band and route tuned together for their own sakes" — that is still two objectives in a trench coat. The load-bearing chain is band → range → clustering → route → arrival → rate → dwell → feasibility, closing back on band. Restore cuts 1–3 and you cannot pick the band without knowing the route, or the route without knowing the clustering the band induces.

The test for whether you built a system or a stack is a one-line ablation: cut the band→range edge and see whether the result moves. If it doesn't, you added a layer. Run it either way and report it — a negative there is far cheaper to discover now than in review.

Q4

How do RF and navigation connect back to the FL job scheduler?

Bilevel, three edges

This is the best question in the document, and its answer is your architecture figure. Frame it as a bilevel problem with a bidirectional interface:

EdgeDirectionWhat crosses itWhere it lands in the code
Demand Scheduler → RF/Nav Which devices need service, how urgently (deadline), expected update value (readiness × effectiveness). This is the lower layer's objective function. S3 deadline + bucket classify
Coupling RF ↔ Nav Band sets range, range sets clustering and dwell; route sets which bands are usable at which stop, and the phase you find on arrival. cuts 1–3 above
Feasible set RF/Nav → Scheduler Not a route — a feasible set plus a cost vector. "These contacts are reachable, at these costs, with this predicted link quality." The scheduler consumes it as a hard gate. S3b gate · features.py slot 10

You already have both sockets for the return edge, which is what makes this a rewiring rather than a rewrite. S3b is where the feasible set lands; slot 10 is where the link-quality estimate lands. Slot 10 today carries a mission-mean scalar — make it a per-contact predicted SNR at estimated arrival time and the same change that closes cut 2 also makes the feature discriminative instead of constant across the batch.

The formal statement, which is also the sentence for the paper: the scheduler is the outer problem (which devices are worth acquiring), RF-plus-navigation is the inner problem (can we reach them, at what cost), and the coupling is bidirectional because value depends on cost and feasibility depends on which devices you selected. Note that this preserves your architectural guarantee untouched — the hard gates still run before anything learned ranks anything.

Q5

Is this enough to justify RL over a cross-heuristic?

It justifies testing, not asserting

Honest answer: the coupling justifies testing whether RL is needed. It does not guarantee RL wins — and the useful move is to stop needing it to.

Whoever told you to check the cross-heuristic is right, and you should build it. But it is your baseline, not your threat. Without a strong joint heuristic, "RL helps" is unfalsifiable and reviewer 74C's complaint never closes. With one, you can state a boundary — and a boundary is a better result than a win.

The claim to aim for is not "RL beats heuristics." It is:

Here is where a decomposed heuristic provably stops working, and we locate that boundary empirically.

Your revision plan already has this instinct at §7.3 — "the learned selector is not necessary under unconstrained conditions; its benefit becomes significant when budgets are tight." And your A1/A2 result is already a paper written in that voice: the mule loses on a clean backhaul, wins decisively under jitter, reported as a crossover surface rather than a pooled mean. Do the same thing for RL and you inherit a rhetorical structure that has already survived scrutiny.

The test

Three properties, each measurable

Design the experiment to measure these rather than assert them. If all three hold and the cross-heuristic still wins, the paper is still good — it becomes when does learning pay for itself in mule-based FL?, which is publishable, honest, and much harder to attack than a contested effect size.

PropertyThe claimHow to measure itWhat falsifies it
a · Non-
decomposability		 Greedy band-then-route is measurably worse than joint search. Offline oracle on small instances (N ≤ 6, exhaustive over band × permutation). Report the optimality gap of each decomposition. Gap ≈ 0. Then the problem genuinely decomposes and you should ship the heuristic.
b · Delayed
consequence		 A band choice at mission m affects the round closing at m+2. Sweep the credit-assignment horizon — train with discount γ near 0 (myopic) up to γ near 1, and plot performance against it. Flat curve in γ. Then there is no temporal credit to assign and a one-step rule suffices.
c · Non-
stationarity		 No single static weight vector wins across regimes. Tune the cross-heuristic's weights per regime and show the winning weights change across the clean/jittery × tight/slack grid. One weight vector wins everywhere. Then publish the heuristic — that is a cleaner result than RL.

Keep the existing claim rule — bootstrap CI excluding 0 and paired Wilcoxon p < 0.05, with the multiplicity position stated explicitly. It is what makes the A2 result credible and it will do the same work here.

Sequence

What to do, in order

Ordered by dependency, and deliberately front-loaded with the cheap measurement changes — two of the six require no new architecture, and either could resolve the question before you build anything.

  1. Fix the measurement no new code τ = 0.82 time-to-accuracy, and run where S3b binds (~60 s, not 120 s). May reveal signal the current setup structurally cannot see.
  2. Wire band → rf_range_m → S3a clustering small · cut 1 Creates the coupling. Immediately testable by ablation, and the ablation is the evidence that you built a system rather than a stack.
  3. Make slot 10 per-contact predicted SNR at arrival small · cut 2 Closes the anticipation edge and fixes the constant-feature problem in one change.
  4. Put travel time and rate-dependent dwell into the budget medium · cut 3 Port Exp 3's cost model into the Exp-4 path; replace the constant session_time with a function of achieved rate.
  5. Build the joint cross-heuristic as a first-class arm medium Alongside B1 (MAX-AoI) and B2 (Oort statistical utility), sharing transport, realism and seeds so the comparison isolates the policy.
  6. Then decide on RL, using (a)–(c) as the criteria gated on 1–5 Not before. Every step above is worth doing whichever way the decision goes, which is what makes this ordering safe.

Scope

This is E3, not a new front

Worth saying to the team explicitly, because it changes what the redefinition costs. Your holistic revision plan already records that E3 — the scheduling and scale experiment — is "the largest untouched block of work in this plan," and Freeze D4 already settles that E4 makes no RL claim and that the RL question belongs to E3.

So this redefinition is not opening a new thread. It is finally specifying the experiment your own plan reserved for exactly this question. The practical consequence: the ICNC submission does not have to carry it. E4's crossover surface stands on its own as an availability result, and the RL question ships with E3 on its own timeline.

One thing to protect while you do this: the honest framing that already makes the E4 result credible — HERMES is not a general improvement on federated learning; it is an availability mechanism, and it costs throughput when availability is not the problem. The same discipline applied to RL is what will make the boundary claim land.
