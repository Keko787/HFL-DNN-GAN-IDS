\section{Evaluation Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Against the State of the Art}
\label{sec:res:sota}

Table~\ref{tab:exp5_headline} sets FeRRy against every baseline at $N=6$. With
one mule at the knee budget, F reaches $\tau$ in 194\,s, and each of the seven
baselines needs 264--282\,s: F is 27--31\% faster, and every comparison is a
claim. FedEx with its own merge (D4$_{\text{FedEx}}$), a variant of D4, is as
slow (281\,s) but misses $\tau$ in one trial, and its comparison is not a claim. Paired seed by seed, F reaches $\tau$ first in 80--90\% of the trials
against each baseline. FeRRy's other two arms are as fast: FX reaches $\tau$ in
178\,s and FQ in 180\,s, and neither differs significantly from F. FQ, flown
after the pre-registered study, is also faster than D4 and E3 at the knee in
its own exploratory family (Holm $p<0.001$). Under the stress budget, only the FedEx route (D4) is
significantly slower. The other baselines trail by 28--60\,s, but they keep
within the budget by leaving devices out, which widens their spread. With three
mules every scheduler reaches $\tau$ in 52--65\,s, except FedEx with its own
merge (D4$_{\text{FedEx}}$), which needs 101\,s: its unweighted accumulation of
one-tour-old updates slows learning. Without a mule (H0), federated learning
over the degraded live link merges 0.5 updates per round and ends at an
accuracy of 0.686, barely above the 0.667 of labelling every flow benign,
against F's 3.5 updates and 0.831.

\subsection{Why FeRRy Is Faster: Reach}
\label{sec:res:mechanism}

F does not need fewer rounds than the baselines: all of them reach $\tau$ in
1.65--1.84 rounds on average, with near-identical update yield. F's rounds are
shorter. Fig.~\ref{fig:exp5_mechanism} shows where a mission's time goes. Every
baseline flies the wide band, which reaches a device only from nearby, so it
spends about 140\,s of a mission in transit between stops. F's plan chooses the
narrow band in 89\% of missions: its range covers the field from about one
stop, so F spends 15\,s in transit and 107\,s dwelling at the lower rate, and
its missions are 22--26\% shorter. FX and FQ inherit the plan's reach: they fly the
narrow or medium band on 95\% and 98\% of missions and spend the same 15\,s in
transit (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp5_learning}b). Pinning F to each band class isolates this
effect: pinned to the wide band, F reaches $\tau$ in 265\,s, D4's own time;
pinned to the narrow band, in 196\,s, F's own. (The band-pinning study's pre-registered
metric is the age of updates, on which the pinned bands do not differ; these
times to $\tau$ are descriptive.) The choice also adapts
(Fig.~\ref{fig:exp5_bands}). As the field fills and the budget tightens, F
shifts to the faster medium and wide classes, mostly wide at $N=24$ under the
stress budget, where the narrow band's dwell would no longer fit.

\subsection{Scale and Decision Cost}
\label{sec:res:scale}

Table~\ref{tab:exp5_scale} follows the comparison to larger fields. At $N=24$, F
is significantly faster than H1 and D3 at the knee (749 against 951 and 911\,s)
and ties the FedEx route (752\,s); under the stress budget it beats the FedEx
route (661 against 752\,s). At $N=12$ no comparison is a claim; FQ, flown in the
same cells in the learned-score study's full-system check, takes 461\,s at the knee and 389\,s under
the stress budget, not significantly different from FX (424 and 386\,s). FeRRy's planning
stays affordable (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp5_systems}a): the planner takes 0.04\,s per
plan at $N=6$ and 1.5\,s at $N=96$ (p95 2.0\,s), a small share of a mission.
In FerrySim, the plan serves 54--57\% of the devices at each scale's knee
budget from $N=24$ to $96$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp5_systems}b). Adding mules divides
the work: at $N=12$, F's time to $\tau$ falls from 486\,s with one mule to
226\,s with two and 151\,s with three (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp5_systems}c).

\subsection{The Design Claims}
\label{sec:res:claims}

Table~\ref{tab:exp5_claims} tests each claim against an alternative that
removes or replaces one part of FeRRy's design. Five tests hold. The coverage
term keeps rounds closing: without it, the round-close rate falls from 99\% to
74\% (C2). FeRRy keeps updates fresher than the fairness baselines under the
stress budget: its network age of updates is 0.78 missions, against 1.06 for
MAX-AoI and 1.07 for Cui's Whittle index, and 1.10 without the coverage term
(C5). On the time-varying backhaul, the adaptive controller lowers it from
0.88 to 0.69. That controller observes the SNR that decides each upload's loss, so this is close to the most that adapting the
backhaul can give. Several parts of the
design show no measurable effect at these settings, and we report them as such.
Pinning the band does not change the age of updates (its effect is on time). The dwell term and the age cap change nothing
over four missions. The merge rules tie: with every device served in Pass~2,
collected updates almost always have merge age zero, so the age weighting
rarely acts; FedBuff's buffering slows learning, and FedProx is not
significantly faster. The selection literature's deadline forms miss slightly
more deadlines than FeRRy's per-device deadline (47\% against 42\% under the
stress budget), but not significantly.

\subsection{Learning Inside the Plan}
\label{sec:res:rl}

The learned-score study's result is flat: no $\gamma$ beats $\gamma=0$ by the pre-registered
margin, and the best learned score ($\gamma=0.75$) matches FeRRy's fixed rule
(FX) while trailing the one-step greedy rule. In the full system FQ is again
indistinguishable from FX at $N=12$ (C4 in Table~\ref{tab:exp5_claims}), as it is
from F and FX at $N=6$ (Table~\ref{tab:exp5_headline}). FX is therefore FeRRy's
per-stop rule, and the null is reported here. The two do not decide on the
same information: FQ picks its next stop on arrival, from the priced dwell, and
FX after serving, from the realized clock, so
the comparison is of two rules, not of two rankings over the same choice.

\paragraph{Learning inside the plan, against learning without one}
The exploratory comparison asks how a learner inside FeRRy's plan fares against
a published learned scheduler. FQ, FeRRy's plan with the $\gamma=0.75$ score,
flew the scheduler comparison's one-mule cells on the same 20 seeds as E3, our single-agent DQN after
Chen et al.~\cite{chen2023model}, which picks every next stop with no dock-time
plan (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp5_learning}). At the knee budget FQ reaches $\tau$ in 180\,s
against E3's 280\,s, a paired difference of 100\,s (95\% CI [63, 141]; Holm
$p<0.001$ across the eight exploratory comparisons). Under the stress budget, FQ
is faster over the 19 seeds in which both reach $\tau$ (174 against 211\,s), but
the CI includes zero. FQ is indistinguishable from F and FX at both budgets. Its
advantage therefore comes from the plan, not from learning. FQ inherits the
plan's choice of band (narrow or medium on 98\% of missions) and spends 15\,s of
a 155\,s mission in transit, whereas E3 flies the wide band and spends 144\,s of
a 228\,s mission in transit. The same ordering holds in FerrySim, where each
learner is scored on its own objective: E3's held-out return falls 6\% below
FX's, while FQ's matches FX's. We did not build a single learner that also makes
the plan's decisions, so this is our closest test of a learner inside FeRRy's
plan and gates against one without them. It supports the two-clock structure
(C4), not a gain from learning.

\subsection{Robustness and Limitations}
\label{sec:res:robust}

Table~\ref{tab:exp5_robust} varies the data, the channel and the devices'
compute. With strongly non-IID data (Dirichlet $\alpha=0.1$), every arm reaches
$\tau$ in only about half of the trials, and F remains significantly faster than
H1 and D3 where both reach it. The radio study flies the time-varying backhaul
throughout, so its default column (F at 216\,s) is not the main configuration
(194\,s). Under its harsher channels F stays ahead of H1 without a claim, and
FX's per-stop rule is significantly faster than F on the default channel and
with 8\,dB shadowing. Two limitations stand out.
First, FeRRy's plan prices the mean SNR, so under the stress budget 30\% of its
missions overrun the budget, by 8.5\,s on average
(Fig.~\ref{fig:exp5_budget}); the FedEx route, which never skips a device,
overruns in 85\%. Second, when devices take time to train (the training-time study), F's
update yield falls from 3.5 to 0.7 updates per round and it reaches $\tau$ in
only 70--75\% of trials, while H1, D4 and D5 keep 85--100\%: FeRRy's plan does
not price training time, so it reaches devices whose updates are not ready.
Adding a compute term to the plan is future work.
